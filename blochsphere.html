The Qubit's Sphere

Every possible state of a single qubit is a point on this sphere — the north pole is |0⟩, the south pole is |1⟩, and everywhere else is a superposition of both. Apply gates and watch the state rotate; drag to look around; measure to collapse it back to a pole.

What you're seeing: the arrow is the qubit's actual quantum state, not a probability cloud — a single point, anywhere on the sphere's surface. X, Y and Z flip the state 180° around their respective axes. H swaps the roles of "certain" and "uncertain": it takes |0⟩ to an equal superposition sitting on the equator. S and T are smaller rotations around the vertical axis — they change the qubit's phase without touching how likely a 0 or 1 outcome is. Measuring always returns a classical 0 or 1, with probability set by how close the arrow sits to each pole — P(0) = (1+z)/2 — and the arrow snaps to whichever pole was found, exactly the collapse this book keeps coming back to.
