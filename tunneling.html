What you're seeing: the glowing curve is the exact steady-state probability density for an electron wave meeting a rectangular barrier — oscillating on the left (incoming plus reflected wave), decaying inside the barrier, and continuing as a smaller, uninterrupted wave on the right. The transmission probability isn't approximated here; it's the standard closed-form result for a rectangular barrier, T = 1 / (1 + V₀²sinh²(κL) / 4E(V₀−E)), with κ = √(2(V₀−E)). Notice how fast T collapses as the barrier gets only slightly wider or taller — that exponential sensitivity is exactly why tunneling matters for a scanning tunneling microscope's atomic resolution, and why doubling a transistor's insulating layer does far more than double its leakage.
