The Cat in the Box

The same mechanism that closes the double-slit's interference pattern is what keeps a cat from actually being alive and dead at once. Every stray air molecule that bounces off the cat quietly picks up a trace of which branch it's in — and the fringe pattern in the corner is the exact same fringe pattern from Chapter 1, being erased the same way, one bounce at a time.

What you're seeing: the cat starts in a genuine superposition, and the fringe panel starts at full visibility — both alive and dead, really, the way both slits are really open at once. Each environment particle that interacts with the cat picks up a little which-branch information, and the coherence remaining after N such hits is exactly cos²θ multiplied N times, cos²θ raised to the Nth power — the identical exponential-decay law that governs decoherence generally. Once coherence is gone, the cat isn't a blur of both outcomes anymore; it's a coin already flipped and sitting face-down, definitely one or the other, and "measuring" it (opening the box) just reads off a fact the environment already settled. That is the honest resolution — no observer required, no mystery, just an enormous number of air molecules doing their job.
