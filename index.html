Two Slits, One Question

Fire particles at a barrier with two slits, one at a time. Watch the pattern that builds up on the screen behind it — then turn on the which-path detector and watch the interference disappear. This is the experiment the book keeps coming back to.

What you're seeing: with the detector off, each particle's amplitude passes through both slits and interferes with itself — the buildup forms bands. Switch the detector on, and which-path information becomes available; the interference term drops out and the pattern collapses to two plain bands, one per slit. The distinguishability slider blends between the two, exactly the way partial which-path information produces partial interference in the book's account of the experiment.
