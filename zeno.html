What you're seeing: the curve plots survival probability after a fixed total time T, as a function of how many equally-spaced measurements are packed into that stretch — N=1 means "don't look until the end," large N means "check constantly." The system here is modeled as a driven two-level system with an internal oscillation and a leak into a lost channel, the same kind of setup used in the actual 1990 ion-trap experiment that first demonstrated this effect. However hard the leak channel pulls, checking often enough always wins: the curve climbs toward certain survival as N grows, because any such system's short-time behavior is quadratic, not linear, in the gap between checks — the mathematical root of the Zeno effect. The book's anti-Zeno effect is real, but it needs a genuinely different kind of system — a continuous reservoir of decay channels, not a single leak — and isn't shown here.
