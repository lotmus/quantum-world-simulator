Beat the Classical Limit

Alice and Bob each get a random question (0 or 1) and must answer 0 or 1, with no way to communicate once the round starts. They win if their answers agree — unless both questions were 1, in which case they need to disagree. No strategy Alice and Bob agree on in advance, however clever, can win more than 75% of rounds — unless they're each holding one half of an entangled pair.

Alice & Bob's strategy Classical: best possible pre-agreed strategy, no entanglement. Quantum: they share an entangled pair.
What you're seeing: every round, a referee flips two independent coins — one question for Alice, one for Bob — and each must answer using only their own particle and their own question, with no communication. Classically, the best possible pre-agreed strategy tops out at 75%, no matter how clever: that is the content of Bell's theorem. With an entangled pair and the right measurement angles, Alice and Bob's answers stay correlated in a way no shared classical script can reproduce, pushing the win rate to cos²(22.5°) ≈ 85.4% — the Tsirelson bound.
